Sensors ​

A sensor is a part placed like any other; its component type says what the targets simulate and what navigation does with its data.

TypePublishesFeeds
Lidar/scanThe costmaps and the localizer
Lidar3D/lidar3d/points, its middle ring on /lidar3d/scanThe costmaps; the ring stands in for a laser
DepthCamera/depth/image_raw, /depth/pointsThe costmaps; a scan made from its cloud stands in for a laser
RGBD/rgbd/image_raw, /rgbd/depth/image_raw, /rgbd/points, /rgbd/camera_infoAs for a depth camera
Camera/camera/image_raw, /camera/camera_infoNothing; the images are yours
IMU/imuNothing; the readings are yours
Ultrasonic/ultrasonic/rangeThe costmaps' range layer
Bumper/bumper, the contacts it feelsCheck_contact

The first sensor of a type gets these topics; a second one of the same type is suffixed with its component name. Each sensor's frame is its component name.

Which sensor navigation uses ​

Nav2's costmaps mark what every point cloud sees, between 0.05 and 2 m above the ground. The costmaps and the localizer also read one planar scan, chosen in this order:

  1. the first Lidar;
  2. the middle ring of the first Lidar3D;
  3. a scan made from the first depth or RGBD camera's cloud, projected onto the ground plane.

A range finder's reading marks the costmaps' range layer. A robot with no range sensor at all is warned that it navigates blind.

Specifications the targets fill in ​

A library part does not yet state a sensor's specification: its range, beam count, field of view or rate. Until it does, the targets use these, and declare each one in compile.json:

TypeSimulated as
Lidar360 beams over the full circle, 0.3 to 12 m, 10 Hz
Lidar3D16 rings of 360 beams over 0.52 rad, 0.3 to 20 m, 10 Hz
Camera, DepthCamera, RGBD320 by 240 pixels, 1.089 rad field of view, depth 0.1 to 5 m, 10 Hz
IMUIdeal, 100 Hz
UltrasonicA 0.52 rad cone of 9 by 9 beams, 0.02 to 4 m, 20 Hz; the nearest return more than 0.05 m above the ground is the reading
BumperAn ideal contact switch over its collision box, 50 Hz

Mounting ​

A sensor looks along its own +x. The chassis connector it is glued to, together with the connector's up, decides which way that points on the robot; the TF tree carries the result, so a sensor mounted upside down still reports correctly in its own frame.
