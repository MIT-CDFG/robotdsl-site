# Lynx forward OAK-D Pro rail boom

A compact diagonal boom carries the USB OAK-D Pro over the sloping front cover. Two M5 slide keys hold its shoe on the left roof rail. The open webs leave the center of the cover free, and a rear frame matches the camera's existing 75 mm M4 hole pattern. This is a fabrication candidate; physical retention and loads are not qualified.

## Make and assemble

Print one boom in a qualified PA12/SLS process. PETG is a fit prototype pending creep and load review. The body is 128.21 × 194 × 55 mm; its main underside can face the build plate, with support beneath the raised camera frame if the process needs it. Finish the M5 clearance holes to 5.5 mm and camera holes to 4.5 mm.

Machine two steel slide keys from the separate STEP. Tap **M5 × 0.8 through** after machining. The STEP's 5 mm bore is a simplified final thread envelope, not a tap-drill specification; the machinist must select the appropriate tap drill and thread tolerance. Do not print the metal keys or screws.

Use two ISO 4762 M5 × 12 screws with 10 mm OD × 5.3 mm ID × 1 mm washers, and two M4 × 8 screws with 9 mm OD × 4.3 mm ID × 1 mm washers. The nominal M4 insertion into the camera is 3 mm; confirm the camera's actual thread depth before fitting these screws.

The fixture is specific to the Lynx 1.0 UC01 left horizontal rail. Slide the keys in from an accessible rail end, removing the vendor end cap or rail joint as necessary. Rail-end access and reassembly need a physical check. The assembled keys sit under the 2 mm rail lips. Nominal M5 engagement is 3.5 mm and the screw tips clear the source slot floor by 0.5 mm. Confirm preload and strength for the selected material.

The counterbores leave straight Allen-driver access from above. Camera screws enter from the rear. The side USB socket remains exposed. Remove the boom when required for cover service.

## What is and is not checked

The camera clears the body mesh by about 52.65 mm. This is a nominal geometry check, not a manufacturing tolerance or operating test.

Host-to-boom retention stays unknown. The downstream M4 camera fastening inherits that unknown upstream retention. No host holes or threads were invented. Loads, vibration, creep, optical task coverage, weather sealing, power and data remain unverified.
