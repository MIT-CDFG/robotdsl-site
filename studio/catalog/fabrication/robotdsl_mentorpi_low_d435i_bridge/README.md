# MentorPi A1 low D435i bridge

Two-foot gusseted bridge on the native MentorPi camera step, carrying D435i forward and below its original camera seat. Retains the two-layer front step and rear M3 camera fastening. Physical fit, omitted electronics and motion remain limited by source evidence.

This low forward bridge uses the real two-hole front camera step. There are TWO stacked 1.5 mm native plates at these holes. The 2.5 mm printed feet, 0.5 mm washers on both outer faces and separate M2.5 nuts are held with M2.5 x 10 screws. The stepped native structure is preserved. Fit the step hardware before the camera, reaching nuts below the front step. The maker mesh omits the motors, steering servo and boards; absence in CAD does not prove internal tool access on the kit.

Two gusseted side arms carry the native D435i rear face. Rear M3 holes are 45 mm apart, at Z7.5 mm. Use M3 x 8 rear screws and 0.5 mm washers through the 3.5 mm upright, giving nominal 4 mm insertion. Check the camera's permitted insertion before tightening. The webs stop above the rear screw-driver corridors; no thin residual rib is left around those corridors.

The camera is ahead of the stock bumper and centered between the two front wheels. The native model's approximate tread-centre steering axes are screened through +/-0.785 rad. This bounded geometric check does not replace a physical full-lock check with the actual servo/kingpins. Access volumes are reserved outside both camera ends; choose a USB cable within that space, then verify its plug and bend radius.

## Fabrication

Print only the listed polymer support, in PA12 using SLS. Nominal holes need a physical gauge/fit check; do not silently scale the whole part. Use separate purchased metal hardware. These are fabrication candidates, not physically tested kits. No strength, creep, vibration, screw preload, impact, environmental or operational qualification has been performed. Electrical supply, data/control links, OS/driver, calibration and first operation are outside this mechanical fixture. Preserve unknowns in the robot report.
